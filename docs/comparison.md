- **Fact:** Dhaka already has several route and fare tools, including Jaatra, Smart Route BD, Dhaka Bus Route, Jatayat, and Vara Koto.
- **Fact:** Jaatra now advertises Pulse, where riders share updates about traffic, crowding, closures, and other conditions.
- **Fact:** A Dhaka hackathon project, RouteGPT, has already demonstrated a Banglish chat assistant for route and fare queries.
- **Inference:** A general Dhaka transit app or chatbot would substantially rebuild existing work.
- **Unknown:** None of the public descriptions establishes complete accuracy, reliable coverage, or whether the remaining gaps are important to enough commuters.

# Compare existing approaches against our needs

## 1. What this comparison covers

**Quick summary:** This comparison tests whether the identified needs justify new work. It uses [the decomposition](decomposed_problem.md), [the needs](needs.md), [the intervention alternatives](interventions.md), and [the research review](background_research.md).

**VERIFIED FACT:** Current product pages and listings describe functions. They verify what the providers advertise, not the real-world accuracy or sustained use of those functions. Links below point to provider listings, primary documentation, official records, or research sources.

**REASONABLE INFERENCE:** “Already covered” means a documented approach addresses much of a need. It does not mean that every trip works, all passengers can access it, or its information is correct today.

**UNKNOWN:** We have not installed and tested each app, checked routes against the street, measured report quality, interviewed commuters, or surveyed how people currently choose among these services.

## 3. What parts of the problem are already well solved?

**Quick summary:** Static route discovery, bus/metro maps, fare lookups, multimodal comparisons, and conversational query prototypes are established. Some parts already have Dhaka-specific offerings.

- **VERIFIED FACT:** Jaatra advertises route search across bus, MRT-6, rail, and walking; fare information; transfer guidance; and, in its latest update, rider reports about traffic, crowding, and closures. [Jaatra App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/jaatra-dhaka-bus-transit/id6809208687)
- **VERIFIED FACT:** Smart Route BD and other local apps publish Dhaka route/fare information. Vara Koto provides local fare and distance estimates. [Smart Route BD](https://smartroutebd.com/en/about), [Vara Koto](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.monixsoft.vara_koto)
- **VERIFIED FACT:** A local hackathon project has demonstrated a Banglish transport chatbot grounded in a structured route database. [RouteGPT](https://devpost.com/software/routegpt)
- **VERIFIED FACT:** Open standards and open-source routers already support connecting route data to trip search. [GTFS](https://developers.google.com/transit/gtfs/), [OpenTripPlanner](https://www.opentripplanner.org/)
- **REASONABLE INFERENCE:** These make “put bus routes, fares, and a chat box on a map” a weak novelty claim. A new user interface alone may not solve a distinct unmet need.

**UNKNOWN:** We do not know whether these offerings are accurate enough for a particular route or user segment, whether their databases are maintained at adequate frequency, or how many target commuters use them.

## 4. What remains inconvenient, expensive, unreliable, inaccessible, slow, or difficult?

**Quick summary:** The evidence points less to an absence of apps and more to the cost of keeping information accurate and useful. Coverage, freshness, trust, and physical transport conditions remain difficult.

| Remaining difficulty | Evidence status | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine live bus positions | **Verified gap in Jaatra's own disclosure:** positions are simulated pending authorized operator GPS; Smart Route BD describes simulated metro movement | A moving marker may look like a live feed when it is not |
| Current and complete bus routes | **Verified as a continuing challenge, not quantified:** local products rely on manual curation; public feedback asks for missing routes and product teams invite corrections | A route planner can fail at the exact origin, destination, or corridor the user needs |
| Student fares across all legs | **Unknown:** products advertise fares, but reviewed material does not establish correct cross-mode student eligibility and minimums | A total can be wrong even if every individual route is correct |
| Variable rickshaw/CNG prices | **Partial:** Vara Koto labels its result approximate; sources do not establish current, representative surge ranges | A single “exact” price creates false confidence |
| Incident confirmation and expiry | **Partial:** Jaatra Pulse now accepts rider updates, but reviewed release notes do not specify how reports are independently corroborated, decay, or get resolved | Sharing reports is not the same as verifying them |
| Banglish query understanding | **Unknown/partial:** English/Bangla interfaces and RouteGPT’s Banglish chat are advertised; no benchmark or failure rate was found | Romanized spelling and mixed language can still cause wrong origin/destination extraction |
| Service availability and wait time | **Unknown:** route listings do not establish real-time arrivals for every provider/route | Static routes cannot promise a vehicle is currently running |
| Physical disruptions | **Fact:** apps and data cannot drain a flooded road, create a vehicle, or make an unsafe transfer accessible | Information may improve a choice without removing the underlying transport harm |
| Non-smartphone access | **Partial:** offline apps and paper signs/help are possible | Battery, device ownership, data access, language, and disability may still restrict use |
| Trust in authoritative sources | **Unknown:** app presence does not establish that travellers know who maintains a fare, route, or report | Unclear source and update date can undermine trust |

**REASONABLE INFERENCE:** A better route calculation cannot fix a route that is physically blocked or a missing operator feed. A new app could make the information burden worse if it adds another stale source.

## 5. Which limitations matter specifically for our target users?

**Quick summary:** The most consequential limitations differ by traveller. A student’s main risk may be a wrong total; an unfamiliar traveller may need the right boarding point; a passenger facing a blockage needs current, relevant information.

| Target user from needs.md | Limitation that matters most | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Student commuter | Whether discounts apply to the right mode and whether all leg costs fit a hard budget | Compare actual student fares paid against displayed estimates and current rules |
| Budget-constrained commuter | Connector estimates may not match quoted fare; the app cannot guarantee a driver’s price | Gather condition-tagged quotes/payments for the same trips |
| Unfamiliar traveller | Missing stops, confusing directions, and manually curated route coverage | Observe real first-time journeys and test apps at actual boarding points |
| Multi-leg commuter | Route existence does not prove the transfer is feasible at that time/place | Accompany travellers and measure walking, wait, missed transfers, and accessibility |
| Traveller affected by rain/blockade | Alerts may lack direction, observation time, or proof they remain active | Audit real incident reports against street conditions and update timelines |
| English/Banglish speaker | Bangla interface may not mean robust Banglish query interpretation | Test natural romanized queries with varied spelling and mixed phrases |
| Passenger without reliable phone/data | App-only guidance may be unavailable at the point of need | Observe device/data access and test paper/staff alternatives |
| Admin or report moderator | Duplicate, contradictory, or stale reports take time to judge | Measure report volume, review time, false positives, and resolution rates |

**Decision:** The research does not support treating all commuters as one persona with one information gap. The target segment and exact failure should be validated before choosing an approach.

## 6. Are we solving a real gap or merely rebuilding something existing?

**Quick summary:** A generic multimodal Dhaka planner is mostly a rebuild. A trustworthy, context-aware street-report workflow may be a gap, but Jaatra Pulse now occupies part of that space, so the remaining gap needs sharper verification.

- **VERIFIED FACT:** Jaatra’s latest release describes real-time rider updates on traffic, crowding, closures, and trip conditions. It also discloses that live vehicle locations are simulated pending operator GPS. [App Store release notes](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/jaatra-dhaka-bus-transit/id6809208687), [Jaatra FAQ](https://www.jaatra.bd/)
- **VERIFIED FACT:** RouteGPT demonstrates that a chat-based Banglish route/fare assistant has already been built as a hackathon project. It is not evidence of an active production service. [Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/routegpt)
- **VERIFIED FACT:** Smart Route BD and Vara Koto already address local route/fare lookup. [Smart Route BD](https://smartroutebd.com/en/about), [Vara Koto](https://varakoto.vercel.app/)
- **REASONABLE INFERENCE:** A pitch claiming “Dhaka lacks a route app,” “nobody has fare estimates,” or “no one lets riders share traffic updates” is contradicted by the current public evidence.
- **REASONABLE INFERENCE:** The narrower possible gap is not simply “crowdsourcing alerts”; it is whether reports are specific, independent, fresh, and sufficiently trustworthy to change a journey, and whether the user can see the evidence and uncertainty.
- **UNKNOWN:** Jaatra’s actual moderation/verification policy, report expiry, contributor coverage, and accuracy in live use. Its release notes establish sharing, not the exact verification process.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether commuters need another separate service, or would benefit more from better data and processes in an existing one.

**Decision:** Do not claim novelty from feature presence. Any later proposal should state which unmet user outcome remains after testing current local alternatives.

## 7. Would a simpler existing approach beat our current idea?

**Quick summary:** Yes, in several cases. If the need is only to find a known bus route, check a regulated fare, or understand one local disruption, a current app, fare chart, operator notice, or trusted human may already be faster.

| User situation | Simpler existing approach that could win | Why it could beat a new system | Where it may fail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knows origin/destination and takes bus | Use Jaatra, Smart Route BD, Dhaka Bus Route, or a printed route map | Already has route data and no new onboarding | Wrong/missing/outdated route; no live service confirmation |
| Needs a bus fare reference | Check an existing BRTA-based calculator or official fare chart | Direct lookup is simpler than an AI conversation | May omit student/mode rules or fare paid in practice |
| Needs a connector fare estimate | Use Vara Koto or ask/agree a price before riding | App gives a baseline; direct agreement gives the actual quote | Estimate is approximate; quote may be high or no alternative driver is available |
| Needs an operational service update | Check operator/station announcement or authority channel | A source responsible for the service may know what changed | Coverage, speed, and clarity vary; channel may be missed |
| Needs a local road observation | Ask a known local contact or check an active community group | Existing network already has people present | Trust, relevance, duplication, and stale posts are difficult to assess |
| Has no smartphone/data | Ask staff/driver or use a posted sign/map | Works offline and at the point of travel | Staff/sign may be absent, inconsistent, or stale |
| Wants to ask naturally in Banglish | Try the RouteGPT demo concept or regular local advice | No need for a new chat product if the existing one answers accurately | RouteGPT deployment/status and answer accuracy are unverified |

**REASONABLE INFERENCE:** A new service must show a meaningful advantage over the easiest available option for a specific target user. More features do not prove that advantage.

## 8. Which opportunities remain worth exploring?

**Quick summary:** Potential opportunities remain around data quality, evidence, user access, and integration with existing choices. These are research directions, not selected features or solutions.

| Opportunity to investigate | Why it may matter | Evidence needed before treating it as a real gap |
|---|---|---|
| Route and stop freshness for a specific underserved corridor | Current apps invite corrections and users report missing routes; route data remains manually maintained | Compare 20–30 actual journeys and route records in existing apps; measure missing/incorrect stops |
| Student fare correctness across combined journeys | Route apps advertise fare estimates, but end-to-end concession handling is unclear | Test actual rules and observed fare payments by mode and student eligibility |
| Variable connector fare uncertainty | Existing estimates explicitly remain approximate | Collect representative quotes/payments with time, weather, trip length, and availability |
| Report trust and expiry | Jaatra Pulse collects rider updates, but verification details are not public in reviewed material | Ask its users/operators how reports are validated; audit report timelines and false/stale alerts |
| Physical transfer and boarding accuracy | Route search does not establish that someone can find/use the transfer | Field-observe unfamiliar and mobility-constrained travellers |
| Non-smartphone and low-connectivity access | Existing offline options help, but point-of-use access remains unknown | Interview target users and observe dead-battery/data-limited trips |
| Shared, reusable local data maintenance | Multiple apps may duplicate manual collection and correction | Compare public data sources, update provenance, licensing, and willingness to share |
| Integration/interoperability rather than another app | Local services already have routes, fares, and now rider updates | Determine whether any provider exposes a correction or data interface and whether users prefer an integrated workflow |

**REASONABLE INFERENCE:** The highest-value next learning step is a comparative field audit of existing services for a narrow user journey, such as a student making a multi-leg trip during rain. That would distinguish a real gap from a feature checklist.

**UNKNOWN:** It is not yet established which one of these opportunities matters most, whether an existing provider is already addressing it, or whether it can be addressed safely within hackathon scope.
